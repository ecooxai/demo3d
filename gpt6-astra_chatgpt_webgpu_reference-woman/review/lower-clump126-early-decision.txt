Recommended next revision: one channel field in the coordinates of each
overlapping FRONT FAMILY, evaluated by every front section that covers it.

Use the accepted center curves to form25 smoothly varying family centers and
widths. Define each family's1–3 unequal longitudinal valley paths in authored
X/Y, so the same valley reaches all overlapping front faces instead of shifting
sideways with each lock's local ellipse. Preserve existing upper geometry,
rear support, contour extrema, center paths, tips, and original materials.

The displacement should aim at the visible union, with an explicit capacity
bound. At a sampled X/Y, let F be the maximum original front depth; each
covering front section has depth h_i and inward capacity c_i before its pinned
rear/contour or minimum thickness is reached. A shared recession D is safe
from being filled when D <= min_i(F-h_i+c_i). Apply the same requested field
to all covering fronts, capped by that safe capacity and each section's own
capacity. A covering surface whose capacity is smaller than D is permitted
only when it still ends below F-D. This preserves the original support while
making the visible channel continuous across overlaps.

Use a broad smooth field, not the raw triangulation's steps or garment folds.
The only early follow-up measurement needed is useful remaining depth and
exposure after the conservative cap. If that bound collapses most depths,
the coherent next architecture is a single closed family envelope replacing
the overlapping internal front faces. Do not compensate with gloss, pigment,
or additional fine ribbons. A family envelope is a wider topology decision
and should be agreed with root before implementation.
