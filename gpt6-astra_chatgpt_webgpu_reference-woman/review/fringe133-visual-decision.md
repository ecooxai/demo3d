# Candidate 133 — independent native visual decision

## Decision

**Adopt `diagonalFringe133: 1` as a small reference-likeness improvement. Keep the overall assessment at 77/100.** The native result improves one specific foreground lock. It does not resolve the broader crown and fringe realism limits.

This decision follows visual inspection of the sole source, `reference/image1.png`, and all fourteen native captures: the off/on front, detail, left, right, back and junction views, plus the enabled first-family-only detail and left profile. The frozen candidate was not changed during this review.

## Visible improvement

The reference has a narrow principal image-left diagonal lock that continues across the outer brow toward the upper cheek, with shorter, unequal companions. In the baseline, the corresponding group thins near the outer eye and contributes to a more even row of brow-height ends. Enabled 133 extends that principal flow down the temple/upper cheek and gives the foreground a clearer long/short hierarchy. It remains narrow enough to leave the eye and cheek readable.

The first-family isolation confirms that this is one continuous bowed group with a restrained side branch. It does not present as a broad ear-covering plate or a set of opaque straps. The fine companions provide a small amount of upper-temple/ear overlap, although the ear still looks much more exposed than in the source.

## Remaining limits

The broad crown still reads as an opaque cap with a sharply bounded part and scalloped release into separate foreground groups. The unchanged groups still have organized lane gaps and a grainy, parallel brush character. The source has more irregular depth and grouping, and its principal diagonal lock is more integrated into the larger root sweep. Candidate 133 does not make a substantial change to those issues.

The source's upper-ear veil is also stronger and more organically interleaved than this candidate's sparse companions. Increasing density or length alone would not justify a higher score; future work must preserve the demonstrated close profile support and avoid a broad rectangular veil.
