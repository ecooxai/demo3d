# Candidate124 native visual decision

## Decision

**Reject124 for production. Retain the accepted groom.** My independent estimate is approximately74/100 for124 enabled, against the current accepted77/100 baseline under Neutral1.4. The wrapper's captured iteration label still displays76; that label does not determine this art judgment.

I reviewed the sole source `reference/image1.png` and all fourteen native images: off/on front, detail, left, right, back and junction, plus the enabled old-cap isolation detail/right. The source, wrapper and audit remain frozen.

## What improved

The foreground has more continuous density across the forehead, and the new paths do not show105's obvious hooks or upward returns. A longer left strand group approaches the source's diagonal direction. The back, lower lengths, shoulder contact and far-ear coverage remain visually stable, consistent with their exact preservation in the source audit.

The old-cap isolation shows that the new roots provide substantial coverage after the old forward overlap is hidden. There is no large background opening in that diagnostic. This validates only the presence of coverage, not its natural appearance.

## Why it is rejected

The new upper coverage resolves as broad, smooth, matte brown plates across the crown. It masks the accepted fine texture and sheen. Narrow retained glossy areas beside the part then form an alternating, stepped pattern against those plates. These defects are prominent in the junction close-up and still visible in the full portrait.

The forehead transition retains a lid-like boundary. The extra families soften the old seven separated groups but introduce many short, similarly tapered brush groups. The left temple boundary becomes a particularly clean diagonal line, whereas the source has overlapping irregular strand flow and fine partial coverage there.

Both profiles show the new dark upper-front surface patch. Its material boundary is more conspicuous than the accepted groom's underlying cap. Neither the preserved lower silhouette nor the numerical reduction in triangles compensates for the more artificial crown.

## Mechanism indicated by the render and source

Hiding the remaining old forward cap changes the main plate/part defects very little. The replacement coverage itself has become the new visible cap; removing more of the old CROWN alpha would not solve that cause.

The new rounded coverage follows the entire upper root trajectory while the original crown remains present. Its inward offset has already returned to zero by path t=.23. Much of the retained crown remains opaque until crown-flow v around.4–.65, so the new roots sit above and mask the accepted upper fine fibers during a long overlap. Their matte fringe material differs substantially from the retained crown/fine response, making the overlap visible as large brown plates and small remaining shiny islands.

The support surface and fine ends do share paths, which is useful. However, simply sharing centerlines does not guarantee that the visible density, material response or upper/lower layering is continuous.

## Proposed next correction, not implemented

Keep124 frozen and test a bounded correction to its overlap before another large rewrite:

1. Bury the new dense root surface while retained crown coverage is high. Let it emerge smoothly only inside the region from which the old cap has already receded. Use the actual crown-flow retention field to control this interval instead of the unrelated early t=.23 release.
2. Keep the accepted upper crown fine flow exposed above that buried overlap. Avoid changing global shine or merely matching the plates' color to disguise their layering.
3. After the overlap is coherent, make the visible foreground read as a few unequal long bowed groups with finer intervening strands, rather than many similarly pointed short groups. Preserve the monotone falling paths and existing lower/ear routes.

A diagnostic hiding the new coverage mesh, or showing its roots alone, could isolate how much the replacement fine strands already support the withdrawn region. The next source correction should follow root's independent diagnosis and an agreed scope.

## Native performance

Root reports native WebGPU true and no page errors for both states. Construction was1826.5ms off and1898.3ms on, an increase of71.8ms. Reported heaps were341.5MiB and335.8MiB. The accepted source geometry count for the enabled case is1,072,144 triangles and51.75MiB. These results remove the immediate concern raised by the slower source-harness construction time, but they do not justify accepting the art regression.
