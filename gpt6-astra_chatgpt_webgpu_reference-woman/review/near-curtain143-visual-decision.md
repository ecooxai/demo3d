## Why the complete candidate is not preferable

The lower half becomes one smooth, strongly synchronized sheet. In the curtain closeup, many fine lines follow almost the same large bend and flatten into two pointed, brush-like finishes. The source photograph has unequal clumps of different widths, layered lower returns and staggered ends. Its connected volume still has intermediate-scale divisions. Candidate143 largely removes that middle level of structure.

The near curtain is also visibly shorter relative to the unchanged far curtain. In the reference, the two lower masses finish at broadly similar levels, with irregular individual ends; the new near finish looks trimmed into two coordinated points. A simple terminal-height perturbation did not produce visibly independent companion bows once all guide surfaces were combined.

A few thin, nearly straight carriers become exposed inside the new lower return. They are small compared with the main sheet, but they make the new clean silhouette less convincing. Their presence is an appearance observation, not a claim that an untested exact triangle intersection was located. The frozen-source protection of a carrier does not prove that it stays visually hidden after surrounding hair moves.

The conspicuous upper angled passage from head-side hair to chest remains unchanged. Candidate143 was deliberately scoped below that passage, so this is a remaining limitation rather than an accidental regression. Combined with the uniform lower sheet, however, it leaves the profile looking authored around a few large curves rather than naturally draped clumps.
