# Iteration156 — connected front crown and emerging fringe

**Status: proposal only, for root review.** No156 geometry or native variant has been authored. Accepted149 remains78/100. The rejected150 source, reports and images remain frozen.

## Shape decision

Build one connected coarse front surface: two unequal banks meet through a shallow part depression, occupy the complete upper transition, and divide gradually into five descending fringe branches. Start with the filled surface and its silhouette. Do not start with individual lifted members and hope their overlap fills the space between them.

I re-viewed the sole reference, the five integrated149 views and150's junction. The photograph's large left sweep continues into the long diagonal without a separate row of visible root starts. Its smaller foreground locks separate at unequal distances from the part. In149, a broad lid ends above separately authored bows. In150, moving narrow roots toward the scalp left a curved skin strip below that lid, while their exposed rounded beginnings formed a ladder along the part. Passing boundary rays did not establish coverage of that strip's interior. A change to width, tint or root-tip position would preserve that failed construction premise.

The156 surface must have a broad, uninterrupted upper interior and a descending course. Its lower openings are the spaces **between continuing branches**, not a common sheet edge above independently starting fringe. A flat filled bank is still a rejection; connected topology is a prerequisite, not the art result.

## One connected construction

**Root distribution.** Use the retained crown volume as the attachment envelope. Copy its boundary positions and first derivative over approximately `.10` units of physical surface distance, then allow a fair departure into the new front. Do not independently project each visible root down onto the scalp as150 did. The actual prepared147 head supplies a clearance floor; it does not require shrinking the entire visible root volume to that floor.

The banks share a shallow, narrow part depression through the upper occupied region. This first coarse trial has no open scalp slit and no separate scalp plate. The crease loses depth before the foreground division; it does not end at a collapsed triangular pole. No individual root cap, rung or rounded tube beginning is exposed along it. Preserve the reference's larger left arch and smaller right bank rather than mirroring their shape.

**Occupied interior.** Author a small ordered control net for the full area between the copied crown attachment and the emerging foreground branches. Adjacent streamlines share vertices and faces through that dense area. There is no missing strip between a retained upper boundary and the lower routes. Where the two banks meet anterior to the part, a connected saddle carries coverage into the central fall; there is no common upward-return endpoint.

**Unequal release.** Continue this same net into five main branches associated with old groups `{0}`, `{1,2}`, `{3}`, `{4,5}` and `{6}`. The left diagonal is the longest foreground continuation; the inner left, central, smaller right and outer right branches have unequal widths and thinning lengths. Open four clefts at staggered physical distances on these routes, approximately across posed head-local `Y=.75…1.08`, rather than cutting all lanes at one row or one height. The exact locations are authored once against the reference and actual head, not a native parameter sweep.

Branch surfaces share the upper net before separating. They have restrained convex cross sections, approximately `.003… .008` depth, with small perimeter returns; they are not72 independently capped tubes. Their lower sides converge smoothly and their ends thin along physical arclength. A branch must continue into its lower bow; do not stop a broad support strip immediately above a separate fine bow. Group0 follows its retained lower trace. The other branches use longer descending controls into their endpoint targets, allowing the released middle routes to move rather than imposing a short corrective hinge.

**Support and normals.** Build one small spatial index from the actual prepared147 front head triangles, with front material-group membership, plus the preserved ear triangles. Fit the control net and branch widths against those surfaces; test emitted triangles as well as centers. In the dense attachment, continuity with the accepted hair envelope takes precedence over unnecessary scalp attraction. In the free forehead section, aim for roughly `.015… .040` clearance behind the branch, subject to the actual finite-width surface. Fit smooth controls; do not clamp individual final vertices in depth. Compute normals from the joined surface, match the copied border normals, and transport frames only after branches separate. Report geometric curvature and parameter derivatives separately.

## Focused gate before the first native pair

1. Confirm finite output, exact retirement and coarse-isolation manifests, and exact protected attributes/RNG. Keep original arrays for restoration.
2. Check the **entire dense surface domain**, not just its perimeter. Its triangulation must cover every intended interior cell up to the four explicitly authored clefts. Check signed orientation, neighbor ordering, edge chords, representative face interiors and actual head/ear clearance. Classify genuinely occluded areas separately from camera-facing areas.
3. Independently test projected interior coverage against the actual head over front and both oblique views, including the region between the retained attachment and branch clefts. Record uncovered area, largest connected hole and maximum gap width. A visible root-spanning skin arc is a failure even if all attachment rays pass. Finite samples still are not a proof of every continuous triangle relationship.
4. Check the normal/tangent collar and the retained group0 trace. Reject an abrupt new surface bank, fold or compressed texture corridor in the source preview; do not rebuild fines to conceal it.

Then send one OFF/ON native wrapper, with the same old fine ranges hidden in both modes and an accepted149 full-detail control. Capture front, detail, both profiles, back and junction. This is an explicitly incomplete coarse art gate, not a new production score.

**Native no-go criteria:** a second lid or common dark hem; any curved scalp strip below the retained crown; a triangular part flap; comb-like exposed roots; parallel straps with repeated spacing; a broad opaque temporal plate; a floating profile bridge, abrupt ridge or visible root/MASS collision; or loss of the accepted long diagonal and ear coverage. Ordinary front/detail must show a useful continuous sweep before junction detail or fine rebuilding can justify further work. Back and protected lower shape must remain unchanged.
