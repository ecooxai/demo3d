# Candidate 129 — native visual decision

## Decision

**Reject candidate 129. Retain the accepted 130 baseline at 77/100.** My subjective whole-portrait assessment of 129 ON is **75/100**. This is an art comparison against the sole reference, not a score inferred from source checks or renderer success.

Reviewed visually: `reference/image1.png` and all twelve `crown129-{0,1}-{front,detail,left,right,back,junction}.jpg` images. The rejected 124 detail and junction were also rechecked to distinguish repaired defects from inherited ones.

## What improved over rejected 124

The burial and coverage mask meaningfully restore the accepted upper crown's fine texture and sheen. The broad matte plates and the conspicuous shiny staircase beside the part are substantially reduced. The upper sweep is consequently closer to the accepted baseline than 124 was. No new conspicuous hole, hook, ear-covering error, or lower-hair contour change appeared in the five principal views.

## Why 129 still loses to the accepted baseline

The crown-to-fringe boundary remains a broad, opaque, scalloped band. The junction view shows the accepted upper fibers terminating into a rounded solid edge, with the new fine foreground groups visibly beginning below that edge. The right side has several distinct blunt lobes; the left side retains a long, unnaturally clean diagonal temple boundary. Restoring the upper crown response did not produce continuous visible root-to-end strand flow.

The new five-family distribution reads as dense, almost straight brush swaths. Two neighboring image-left groups merge into a broad rectangular veil, while a large clear forehead gap separates that veil from the narrow central group. Most ends fade over a similar brow-height region, so they look softly chopped rather than individually falling through several unequal depths. The accepted version is also too organized, but its foreground groups have clearer bows and less broad, flat density.

The source has a long diagonal lock crossing toward the exposed-eye/temple area, an unequal shorter central bow, and much sparser irregular companions. Its density changes continuously along those paths. Candidate 129 concentrates too much fine density into wide, nearly parallel lanes and leaves the major clear gaps more graphic. The full portrait makes this difference visible without requiring the junction close-up.

Both profiles preserve the accepted side and lower drape. The right profile exposes a larger, cleaner forehead opening beneath a blunt crown edge; that does not improve likeness. The back is visually stable. These preservation successes do not offset the frontal regression.

## Structural diagnosis

The native images support the intended explanation for 124's matte upper plates: masking and burying the dense replacement roots fixes most of that particular overlay problem. They also show that this was not the only cap defect.

The remaining visible contour is shared by retained crown coverage and the exposed rounded replacement support. Their overlap remains dense across a correlated release region, so the observer still sees one solid band. This is a coverage and density-transition problem, not evidence that more crown relief or global shine is needed.

Separately, reducing the number of dominant families while preserving broadly uniform lane spreads and similar terminal sampling does not by itself create natural clumps. It produces fewer but wider bands. The desired next structural change would have to alter the visible density along the actual bowed paths and their unequal endpoints, rather than merely changing counts or further translating the same support under the cap. No new candidate is implemented by this review.
