# Display-tone128: independent visual review

**Decision: select mode1, Neutral tone mapping at exposure 1.4. A modest overall 77/100 is defensible if adopted.** This is a display improvement. The current production score remains 76 until root integrates and validates its selected change.

I visually inspected the sole reference, `reference/image1.png`, and all 20 supplied images: `review/display128-{0,1,2,3}-{front,detail,left,right,back}.jpg`. No image measurement, generated imagery, external asset, new test or production edit was used for this review.

Mode1 removes much of the baseline's gray veil from the face and neck. Its peach skin, brown irises and pink lips resemble the reference's color relationships more closely. The cotton reads as white with cool soft shadows, and the darker hair remains distinct from it. The clearer light-blue background also matches the requested default presentation. These gains are visible at ordinary front-view size and remain consistent in both profiles and the back.

Mode1 does expose the existing pinkness across the forehead and the dark outer socket on the far eye more clearly. I considered those costs directly in the detail image; neither is sufficient to prefer the baseline's general desaturation. The cheek transition and lip tone still look more plausible in mode1. The pre-existing crown carrier edge, fine fringe structure, eye/socket registration and broad ribbon-like rear hair remain visible construction limits. A one-point increase is appropriate; this does not establish a large likeness or production-quality jump.

Mode2 adds brightness and pinkness without a useful detail advantage over1. The bright nose/cheek region and cotton lose some tonal separation, making the result feel more cosmetic and chalky. It is not the selected balance.

Mode3 weakens the dark-hair, skin and white-cloth relationships throughout the five views. The rear hair looks especially gray and flat; the face loses the reference's clearer color and tonal depth. I reject it.

The reference provides a frontal photograph. Profile/back conclusions here concern natural display and material separation, not unsupported claims about matching unseen photographic views. No geometry or atlas correction is inferred from a change of display curve.
